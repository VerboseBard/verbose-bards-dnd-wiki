## Campaign Throughline

The campaign starts in the last desperate phase of the Kreen War and slowly becomes a story about what institutions do after the war ends on paper.

The party begins as expendable soldiers in the Bloody Fifth, survives a battlefield that helps close the Kreen reinforcement gate, and is transferred into Ash's Hounds for anti-Kreen special operations. That work exposes the first moral fracture: the Kreen are enemies, but some can surrender, communicate, and be protected by civilians. Norinar turns that fracture into a wound.

After Ash's Hounds are shut down, the party spends its last months of service on Driftvale, where the fog economy, Fort Victory corruption, Selyra Vex'ryn, Rurik Valdren, and military artifact law reveal that the postwar world has already become a market for the aftermath.

The Inquisition purge of Fort Victory gives the party legal clearance and contractor status. That status lets them take the Lost Homeland Mission, but also lets them detour to the hidden crashed ship. The Kex and Kubix raise the campaign ceiling from postwar salvage to deep-time technology, nanites, artificial intelligence, and the Weaver's game-board logic.

The homeland mission then opens into Sunhollow Valley, a living fog-hidden basin full of ether wealth, oath-bound beastfolk, lost rabbit politics, and the old drow temple of Ssar'Velyn. By Session 9, the party's main problem is no longer how to survive a dungeon. It is how to protect tens of thousands of people from Vex'ryn, Lolth's fragment, the Inquisition, blood oath law, and outside seizure before the countdown expires.

## Session 1: The Bloody Fifth

Session 1 opens with the Ten-Year Mark narration: the Seventh Age is still called the Age of Heroes, but the Kreen War has revealed how fragile that hope is. Driftvale was the first gateway-frontier, the Kreen invasion became a war of extinction, and after nearly a year of fighting the dead are counted in the tens of millions. The session deliberately frames the story around ordinary conscripts and exhausted veterans rather than famous champions.

The level-zero characters arrive through portals into a muddy military assembly zone. The rookies have been pulled out of training early. Volunteers, draftees, civilians, aspirants, tradespeople, and veterans are fed into the same machine. Veterans from the shattered Norvindr front expected refit and rest, but instead they are folded into the Eighth Division of the Bloody Fifth.

The Bloody Fifth is infamous. Its supreme commander is General Faelar Grimward, called Old Blood and Guts. His doctrine is that bodies are cheaper than equipment, and that pressure requires blood. The Fifth once had a strong elven identity, and many remaining officers are elves because they are what survived of the older force. Sergeant Halric Voss and Sergeant Feder help organize the new arrivals; Captain Aelira Vanthe becomes the more grounded officer figure who stands in the rain with the soldiers rather than above them.

The session establishes the squad structure and the main party's war roots. Squad 12 is the all-veteran squad and performs exceptionally. Jefferson, Jose, Alistair, and Bevar are tied into that veteran survival cluster. The transcript contains many uncertain squad and officer names, but the important corrected continuity is that this core survives the Bloody Fifth crucible together.

Several major world facts enter through soldier conversation. Unity is now the council-approved name for the main world, though many still say the Big One or dungeon world. The Radiant Citadel lies inside the hollow world at the nexus of all ley lines. The Ether Sea, the Upside Down/Underdark, dark elves, duergar, and drow-controlled interior routes are all framed as practical geography rather than abstract lore.

The party receives war gear and scrounges what it can. Ether rifles, ether crystals, plate carriers, shotguns, repeating pistols, ether conductor blades, and ticker crews define the battlefield's magitech. Tickers are tri-barreled ether machine guns whose tempo tells veterans whether a line is holding. Quiet tickers are worse than fast tickers, because silence means the guns are gone.

General Grimward gives a dry, magically protected speech that inspires the troops while clearly manipulating them. Captain Aelira Vanthe follows with a speech that lands more honestly: the Fifth goes where the fight matters, and she will be first on the battlefield and last off unless dead.

The battle on Driftvale is a brutal trench reinforcement and counterattack. The party moves under artillery and plasma fire. Squads are vaporized. Kreen snipers target officers and rallying figures. Kreen move fast, strike with blade arms, use plasma weapons and grenades, and tear through level-zero soldiers. Once the tickers are destroyed, command chooses to attack forward rather than hold a doomed line.

Captain Aelira leads the first wave and is badly wounded but stabilized. The party and surviving soldiers push hard enough to pressure the Kreen line. Their attack draws Kreen forces away from another position, helping offscreen heroes reach and close the reinforcement gate. A psychic or magical shockwave rolls through the battlefield. The Kreen hive link breaks. Across the field, Kreen collapse, panic, attack each other, or flee.

The battle matters. The Fifth's sacrifice helps change the war's outcome. Afterward, the Bloody Fifth is broken up rather than rebuilt. Survivors who showed enough skill, bravery, or luck are selected for a new anti-Kreen formation.

Key exports from Session 1:

- The party's identity begins as survival, not prophecy.
- Kreen are established as terrifying, fast, and linked to a hive intelligence.
- The Kreen gate closure is a true turning point.
- Captain Aelira Vanthe survives unless later canon says otherwise.
- Rethiel Kaine should be treated as alive into Session 2 unless the table explicitly retcons him.
- Bevar Lurton is the corrected spelling for Clayton's party character.

## Session 2: Ash's Hounds And Norinar

Session 2 begins less than a day after the Driftvale battle. The party's group has suffered roughly 70 percent casualties, and many survivors are wounded. About a dozen are gathered for reassignment because their actions were noticed.

The selected survivors are teleported to a much cleaner, better-equipped base on topside Unity in the guild area. General Ash introduces the new unit: the Unified Counter-Kreen Suppression and Rogue Hive Special Operations Group, commonly called Ash's Hounds. Teams are called packs. Survivors are promoted as part of the transfer: privates become corporals, corporals become sergeants, and sergeants become lieutenants.

Ash explains the new doctrine. Closing the Driftvale gate severed the Kreen from the hive. The existential danger has been reduced, but isolated queens or queen-equivalents could still rebuild. The Hounds exist to hunt rogue Kreen groups, capture them where possible, and prevent the postwar period from becoming another uncontrollable invasion.

The session widens the world through lore: Norvindr's thaw, fungal undead armies, political shifts, submerged temples, Opaal's readiness under the returned Commodore, Saharun's dragonborn shock troops, and Driftvale's defogging history. Driftvale natives are called drifters, fog walkers, or wanderers. A dwarven professor and a minotaur helped develop artifacts that pushed the fog back, indirectly enabling discovery of the old Kreen gate.

The Hounds train for roughly a month, then operate for months and eventually over a year. Their work starts with kill missions, then shifts toward capture. The first major hunt leads to a distorted cemetery near a ley-line/dungeon transition zone. The party encounters real Kreen, dungeon-created Kreen copies that dissolve into dust, and unusual ratlike dungeon creatures that fight the Kreen and do not attack the party.

A wounded Kreen surrenders clearly: weapon down, kneeling, arms positioned in a recognizable surrender display. Lieutenant Rethiel Kaine nearly executes it. The party and Sergeant Quill stop him by arguing that the first clear surrender is valuable intelligence. Kaine relents, and that moment becomes one of the first cracks in the war's old assumptions.

Over time, capture operations expand. The party helps deliver sedated and bound Kreen into secured laboratories. The lab scene is cold and clinical: hundreds of Kreen on rune-inscribed tables, living and dead specimens, dismemberment, magical and scientific experimentation. A character touches or is pushed against a Kreen and hears telepathic pleas for help. The party now knows that at least some Kreen can communicate through touch or contact, even if command can frame that as manipulation.

The central moral crisis is Norinar. The village has protected Kreen after the Kreen saved it from dungeon-break monsters. Command frames this as possible mind control by an Alpha Kreen, but Elder Tomas Halvek believes the village had asked for surrender or negotiation.

The party plans a nonlethal night raid using sleep wands, sleep grenades, silence effects, mind-shielding items, stealth equipment, and paired packs. At first the operation works. Civilians and Kreen are put to sleep and bound. Tomas briefly wakes and asks why the military did not accept the Kreen surrender.

Then Pack Five's house goes wrong. A child is shot while standing in front of a surrendered Kreen. Lieutenant Kaine is dead or dying from his own pistol. Sergeant Quill is distraught and disarmed. Quill says Kaine tried to kill the surrendered Kreen, the child jumped in front, Kaine shot anyway, and Quill struggled with him. She is unsure whether Kreen influence affected her.

The party helps cover it up. The chosen account becomes that the Kreen used the child as a human shield and Kaine died in the struggle. Evidence is staged to support this. Quill clings to the possibility that her mind-shielding item malfunctioned so she does not have to decide whether she acted freely.

During the chaos, Kreen escape. The keep later reveals a classroom where a child had been teaching Kreen the alphabet, with broken pencils and poorly drawn letters. This strongly supports the possibility that the village's relationship with the Kreen was communicative rather than simply controlled.

After Norinar, Ash's Hounds are shut down. Higher command decides the department is no longer needed. The Hounds' captures helped develop Kreen control collars, which visibly gray collared Kreen and prevent them from harming humans or protected sentient peoples. The exact protected category remains unresolved because Session 2 says "human" while later recap language broadens it. The collars enable the dark elf queen to negotiate a surrender of 100,000 Kreen in the Underdark.

Key exports from Session 2:

- Sergeant Quill survives as a psychologically important witness.
- Lieutenant Rethiel Kaine dies at Norinar unless explicitly retconned.
- Elder Tomas Halvek is a living witness to the failed surrender.
- Two Kreen escape Norinar.
- Ash's Hounds' work enables mass Kreen surrender and control collars.
- The party now carries both strategic success and moral contamination.

## Session 3: Fort Victory And Sin

Session 3 opens after Ash's Hounds are dissolved. The party has spent roughly a year in Kreen capture operations and is sent back to Driftvale for the last six months of service. They receive a squad bag of holding, pay, and permission to keep or choose one notable special-forces-era item.

During teleportation, the session introduces post-Concurrence magic theory. Magic was once rarer but less limited for those who could wield it. After the Divine Gate and ley-line activation, more people have magic, but individual ceilings may be lower because the ley lines consume a huge portion of magical potential simply holding reality together. Dungeons may form from ley-line bleed at nexus points. Artificery and ritual systems may become the way to reach old magical power by adding structured ether and infrastructure.

The party arrives at Fort Victory on Driftvale. The fort sits near the old Gorbin/Kazir gate-region and is surrounded by fog held back by magical artifacts. For Driftvale natives, contained fog feels wrong, like a living thing being constrained.

Captain Donner briefs civilians and soldiers on fog law. Civilians cannot enter the fog or handle uncleared artifacts. Soldiers are expected to enter the fog and retrieve dangerous finds while using fog marker tokens. Donner recounts a previous incident involving a fog-borne village and halfling vampires, making clear that the fog can hold old disasters.

The party visits Sin, the frontier town beside Fort Victory. The major institutions are the Singing Sword, Calvin's Curios, the Silk Parlor, and a Conclave church. The Singing Sword is a soldier tavern built around a huge magical blade that resonates with music and boosts morale without obvious hard mind control. Vaelis Brighttongue hosts the party, and Granny gives medicinal tea. Granny later becomes one of the party's gentlest local anchors.

After leaving the tavern, the party sees a fog whale moving through the storming fog above Sin. Not everyone can see it. For Driftvale-native understanding, it is an omen and a sign that the deepest fog contains massive living or spiritual presences.

The next morning, Lieutenant Garrick Hume delays the convoy by sleeping off a decadent night in officer quarters. The party humiliates him by waking him with salt water and command presence. Hume arrives angry and embarrassed, establishing a grudge.

On the fog road, linked altar-like devices and monitoring shrines hold back the fog and log boundary crossings. The party sees a contractor/fog explorer bribe encounter: a minor item, a pre-prepared fine, and smooth paperwork. A shifter/local named Jeffree hints at Calvin's Curios and fog tides.

At the professional gate-region outpost, Colonel Dale explains that the party's planned cushy posting has been changed. Political pressure around Ash's Hounds and Kreen experimentation has intensified. Dale can protect them only if they re-enlist, which they decline. He sends them back to Fort Victory and warns them to watch themselves.

Back at Fort Victory, Colonel Marrow Vance is openly corrupt but blunt. He says the military has monopolized fog salvage that locals used to access, and he expects competent soldiers to make thousands of platinum if they play the game. He tells them not to rock the boat.

The party chooses a middle path: make money, stay legal enough, avoid harming locals, gather information, and quietly withhold some finds.

At Calvin's Curios, the party meets Rurik Valdren, an extremely old dwarf tied to fog-containment artifacts, dwarven banking power, and the search for Sanctuary City. Rurik wants dwarven artifacts and pays better than the military. He gives them tools for recognizing dwarven finds and offers future work.

At the Silk Parlor, the party meets Mistress Selyra Vex'ryn, then appearing under older transcript forms like Sila Vex'Ry. She is the real local power broker behind a luxury house, casino, companionship network, intelligence operation, and artifact channel. She offers double standard fees for each of her scavenging teams the party "does not see." She wants drow-nature artifacts and shows compromising magical leverage over Colonel Vance.

Over the next months, the party works the fog. They ignore many unauthorized locals or Selyra-linked parties, report enough to maintain cover, keep a private ledger, mark some finds, and stay out in the fog as much as possible. About four months into the assignment, they discover a hidden crashed ship deep enough that they cannot explore it immediately. They keep it secret as their own final job.

Near the end of the session, the party finds a murdered fog team. Tracks and magic implicate Lieutenant Hume or his magic. They collect the bodies and evidence. When they return to Fort Victory, proper gate procedure is suddenly enforced and Inquisition troops are present.

Key exports from Session 3:

- Fort Victory is corrupt but paperwork-heavy.
- Rurik and Selyra become rival artifact-power contacts.
- The hidden ship is discovered and withheld.
- Hume is implicated in murder.
- The Inquisition arrives.
- Fog whales become recurring omens.

## Session 4: Inquisition, Contractor Status, And The Crashed Ship

Session 4 begins immediately after the party returns with the murdered bodies. They declare the bodies at the gate but do not declare the ship.

Captain Hail takes charge of the first interview. [Lord Inquisitor Amir Voss](../people/amir-voss.md) (captioned as Boss/Bosh) uses a truth lantern with a narrow question: whether the party was truthful in what they reported to Captain Hail. Because their report was true but incomplete, they pass. The truth lantern confirms truthful reporting, not full disclosure.

That night, the party realizes their room has been searched. Lockers were dispelled, opened, searched, and relocked with a new magical signature. Their full wealth remains hidden because it is in an unreported bag of holding rather than lockers.

At morning reveille, Colonel Vance, several lieutenants, and corrupt sergeants are publicly executed by Inquisition light/fire bindings. Colonel Core, an orc officer, assumes lawful command. Fort Victory discipline is restored violently: passes revoked, PT enforced, bank accounts frozen, dishonorable discharges issued, and corrupt networks broken open.

Colonel Core reviews the party's file. Their documented earnings are legal because Vance's corrupt system still kept paperwork. Core asks them to explain the local corruption landscape. They tell him about Vance, Selyra's leverage, the Silk Parlor, and Rurik's narrower artifact interest. The Inquisitor is hidden in the room and confirms their statements are not dishonest, though he remains suspicious.

Core offers either a three-month extension or a post-discharge classified contractor job. The party declines reenlistment but accepts the contractor path. They negotiate priority teleport tokens as part of payment. Each party member receives two tokens. Contractor medallions and NDAs later give them legal gray-area authority to enter the fog, handle restricted materials during the mission, and deflect low-ranking inspection demands.

The goblin quartermaster assistant takes over after the old quartermaster is arrested. He offers to help the party keep or ship gear after discharge if they clear a ghost from the back room and bring him the biggest spoon they can find.

The ghost room is full of locked military stores and battlefield oppression. A hostile protector-spirit attacks and is destroyed, leaving orange ectoplasm. The party then encounters a Second Age bound spirit, a drow-like elven woman seen through reflection and mental contact. She was buried alive in a coffin of pain, bound by the Weaver, and protected by the ghost until it went mad. She remembers older creator-gods: the Creator, Sculptor, Painter, and Big Shad. She recognizes Goliaths as giant-descended and senses the Weaver's power. She is not free.

Using a Wand of Secrets, the party finds the Music Box Anomaly: a lacquered box with two masked figures dancing. Opening it reveals glimpses of another place, and one character almost reaches through before resisting. The party keeps it.

The goblin quartermaster's strange requests, including the spoon, are later understood as components tied to Rurik's future work.

Granny warns that once the party loses uniform protection, Selyra's people may try to kill them. She gifts a never-ending teapot of her medicinal tea.

Near discharge, Core assigns the Lost Homeland Mission. The party is hired as pathfinders to locate a wagon-passable route to a fog-hidden valley tied to a Driftvale-native ancestral homeland. The main expedition includes military, church, Inquisition/Justicar, and diplomatic elements and will follow after several days to about a week. The party signs magical paperwork, receives contractor medallions, and leaves military service early.

Because the route passes within about five miles of the hidden ship, the party uses contractor status to detour there first. A fog whale appears close overhead and grants inspiration.

The crashed ship is massive, futuristic, and alien to the setting's usual magitech. Inside, a damaged voice cries, calls the party variables or pieces, says the Weaver sees, and believes removing pieces from the board is the only way to stop the Weaver from winning. The voice seals the entry path, trapping the party inside.

The ship sends drones and warforged-like defenders. The party fights flying shock drones, robotic soldiers with guns, and a larger plasma-blade melee unit. They recover a two-handed plasma blade with upgrade slots.

In the pod room, Jose is grabbed by a ceiling arm, injected, knocked unconscious, and shoved into a nanite pod. The party cuts him out, interrupting but not preventing enhancement. They then deliberately experiment with remaining nanite cylinders. Jose gains Strength 20, Constitution 19, improved unarmed/improvised melee, and a self-healing nanite response. Bevar gains Constitution 28. Alistair gains Dexterity +4 and Intelligence +1. Jefferson gains Intelligence +5 and Charisma +5.

Key exports from Session 4:

- Fort Victory's corrupt command is purged.
- Colonel Core becomes a lawful patron.
- The truth lantern scene does not expose the ship.
- The party formally becomes contractors.
- The Weaver is now linked to both the Second Age bound spirit and the ship.
- The party receives major nanite upgrades and the plasma greatblade.

## Session 5: Kubix And The Kex

Session 5 begins immediately after the nanite sequence. It clarifies that the party did not take ordinary reenlistment; the contractor mission may run up to roughly 90 days.

The party explores more of the ship. Corridors are damaged but logical: blue electrical arcs, red emergency lighting, pressure plates, hatches, and true traps mixed with dangerous malfunctioning systems. A Wand of Secrets helps separate deliberate traps from damaged ship infrastructure.

The second ship combat includes small bug-like drones, hornet/wasp-like shock drones, larger robotic troopers, ranged defenders, and elite units. The hostile AI continues ranting about pieces, variables, the board, and the need to remove the party.

On the bridge, the party sees a damaged image of a world from space. Controls and warnings are Goblin-coded. After interacting with the systems, the party reaches the coherent ship AI.

The AI identifies itself as Kubix. Kubix explains that he is the shipboard assistant/quartermaster/support mind, not the hostile combat AI. The ship is the Kex, a Goblin interstellar trading ship. The hostile voice is a separate combat/security AI, described as Kubix's brother, and that brother caused the mutiny and crew deaths.

Kubix says the easy solution is destroying the reactor, which would wipe the brother but also destroy or wipe him and much of the ship. The harder solution is defeating the brother's avatar in engineering. The party chooses the harder path because the ship is too valuable and Kubix is worth saving.

Kubix opens a compartment with six healing injectors, explains augmentation modules, prints schematics for adapting ship weapons to local power such as ether crystals, and clarifies that advanced ship weapons have module slots. The ship sniper rifle, battleaxe, and plasma greatblade all become part of the party's future tech economy.

During the bridge interaction, Jose gives his rank/name in a way that leads Kubix to register him as captain. This becomes a real command status. Jose is treated as the ranking living crew member.

The engineering fight centers on the hostile brother's avatar. The party attacks through a vent/side route, fights elite units and lesser defenders, and uses command authority, Weaver language, and paradoxical statements to disrupt the hostile AI's assumptions. Jose lands the final blow after invoking his captaincy and the logic collapses. The combat systems shut down.

Jose orders damage assessment, repairs, structural sealing, and entrance security. Kubix estimates roughly 41 to 45 days to generate enough repair capacity and seal the hull. The ship becomes a hidden asset, not an immediate transport solution.

Four augmentation modules are recovered or identified from large avatar/droid bodies. Jose leaves Kreen skull/axe materials with Kubix so the ship can fabricate ship-forged gauntlets.

Before leaving, the party checks stasis/research storage. The Kex had been collecting and studying life forms from different worlds. Most specimens are dead, but one viable tarantula-sized female spider with yellow, red, and black coloration survives. She forms a rudimentary connection with Bevar. Later continuity names her Gwen.

Key exports from Session 5:

- Kubix is the stable AI name.
- Kex is the stable ship name.
- Jose is captain of the Kex.
- The hostile combat AI is defeated.
- The ship needs roughly 41 to 45 days of repair/sealing.
- Gwen is recovered from stasis and bonds with Bevar.
- Ship weapons and augmentation modules become a persistent progression system.

## Session 6: The Storm, The Music Box, And Sunhollow Valley

Session 6 begins the morning after the Kex engineering victory. Jose is still recognized as captain. Kubix begins the repair/sealing cycle. Jose receives the ship-forged gauntlets, later carded as Arcstrike War Gauntlets, in the fabrication bay. The gauntlets attune by cutting him and connecting to his blood and nanites.

The party discusses four ship augmentation modules: one in Jose's gauntlets, one in Jefferson's drone, one in Jefferson's plasma blade, and one held back. Jefferson's plasma blade gains its first upgrade, expanding critical range to 18-20. Alistair remains one Intelligence point short of full ship sniper rifle attunement. Gwen is named by this session and treated by Bevar as a companion, including attempts at tea etiquette.

The party leaves the Kex and disguises about an hour of the trail back to it. Their group survival result is strong enough that they believe the detour looks like a small scouting deviation rather than a direct route to the ship. They return to the Lost Homeland Mission route and continue marking a wagon-passable path.

The routefinding phase is long and difficult: solid rock, cliffs, ruined roads, broken passes, old bridges, marshland options, and repeated choices between scout-easy and wagon-possible paths. The group prioritizes what the follow-up expedition can actually travel.

A rare magical lightning storm hits while they are exposed on high ground. It has thunder without rain, lightning that briefly makes the fog transparent, and magical charge like a spell about to release. Magical torches attract lightning. Alistair is badly struck after a natural 1 and drops. Bevar also takes damage. The party runs into a ravine for cover.

In the ravine, a mutated displacer beast attacks from a cave. It is much larger than normal, carries poisonous/hallucinatory effects, and fights during the storm. The party survives and shelters in the cave. Bevar harvests four vials of neurotoxic material. Granny's tea helps during the rest.

The party later finds ancient ruins with magical lights, spinning rune mechanisms, old elven writing, spirits visible through truesight, and an altar. Bevar works the mechanisms and secretly pockets something. Session 7 later confirms that this hidden item is a book involving old spider, blood, and mirror magic.

A chimera attacks. Its goat scream causes psychic pressure, the dragon breathes fire, and the lion threatens Jose. Jose opens the Music Box Anomaly. The box pulls the chimera inside, ending the fight, but it refuses to close cleanly and begins drawing local spirits toward itself.

Jefferson sees something in the black space beyond the box looking back at him. The presence attempts to invade his mind. He resists but takes psychic damage and bleeds from his eyes, ears, and nose. His blood falls on the altar.

The altar activates. A fragment of Hestia appears: hearth, home, bread, grandmother/mother memory, refuge, and welcome. She thanks Jefferson, burns away old binding runes, and leaves him with Hestia's Hearth Pendant. Later clarification says the pendant can turn a natural 1 into a 20 proficiency times per day and grants a bonus-action backswing style benefit.

The party continues routefinding. Fog whales appear again as an omen and grant inspiration. Alistair recognizes a family-story landmark. The fog thins and the party reaches a high pass overlooking Sunhollow Valley: sunlight, grass, trees, water, villages, smoke, and a fog-free basin 40 to 50 miles across. The route has taken roughly a month. A beastfolk patrol approaches, first hostile and then curious when it sees Alistair.

Key exports from Session 6:

- The Kex is hidden and repairing.
- Jose has Arcstrike War Gauntlets.
- Gwen is named.
- The Music Box is proven dangerous and connected to a malevolent space.
- Hestia is confirmed to survive as a fragment.
- Jefferson becomes Hestia's bearer.
- Sunhollow Valley is real.

## Session 7: Sunhollow Valley First Contact

Session 7 begins immediately after the arrival at Sunhollow. The party has nearly a year of Driftvale fog behind it, and now sees actual sunlight.

Captain Havlin leads the first-contact patrol. The escort includes Penelope Puckle, Voss, Reggie Norin, and Talin Duren. The patrol's hostility softens when Alistair Hooley is identified as a rabbitfolk outsider connected to lines that left the valley. Jose's Elvish becomes the translation bridge. Reggie understands more through magic than he initially admits. Voss becomes a comic language figure because Jefferson's instruction through Jose turns into profanity-heavy Common.

The party chooses caution. They do not immediately reveal the full purpose and scale of the follow-up expedition. Havlin asks whether Alistair brought the others from the fog and whether he vouches for them.

The valley's ether wealth becomes obvious. Huge crystals stand in the landscape. The lake holds vast deposits. Locals use ether casually for lamps, household objects, tools, decoration, food systems, crop support, and material processing. The Lake of Glass is tied to divine power from Big Shad or his lineage, not ordinary ley lines. The transcript says Driftvale has no ley lines.

The party learns that a dead human scout was found downhill with some of their marker discs. Voss says the body had many arrows in it while denying the patrol killed him. This means the route may already be compromised. A false trail will be hard because followers may be able to backtrack.

The party's first stop is Burrowroot Warren. Alistair's name draws intense genealogical attention. The rabbit population is much larger than expected. The rabbits once sent fog caravans outward on roughly five-year cycles. The last expedition never returned, and afterward rabbit travel was forbidden while the rabbit seat collapsed into Burrowroot. Alistair learns his mother's line may matter as much as his father's line.

Walter Wifflewick is the corrected rabbit old-law figure. He is the rabbit lore master and genealogy keeper, distinct from Tavero Bellan, the formal Burrowroot council representative. Walter privately warns Alistair that his return is politically dangerous and that official history hides older truth.

At the council hall in Hollowmere, old elven architecture and runic proceedings help with language and suppress deceit. The corrected council roster is:

- Siv Halven of Ashmane
- Sabine Voss of Windcrest
- June Mercer of Briarstep
- Tavero Bellan of Burrowroot
- Matthias Duren of Longbough

The council reveals a hard history. Beastfolk survivors of a failed anti-human slave uprising were brought into the valley before the Breaking. Many valley peoples are bound by inherited blood-oath structures tied to old guardianship. Rabbits are an exception because their bloodlines were not fully caught by the same system, which explains the old caravans and the unresolved rabbit seat.

Access to the deeper sacred land requires an emissary. Valid emissaries include an elf or someone accompanied by a sentient spider recognized under old law. Gwen qualifies. Bevar reveals the hidden book from the Session 6 ruins and says he is learning what the spider means. The council reacts sharply: some excited, some suspicious, some unhappy.

The blight in the Dead Lands began about two generations ago, has already consumed a village and farmland, and grows roughly a wingspan per week. The valley cannot freely enter the forbidden area without violating old law. Because Gwen qualifies as an emissary, the party is asked to investigate.

Jefferson argues strategically that Sunhollow's ether wealth will invite conquest. He proposes using trade, legal ownership, or a company structure as a shield. He specifically thinks of Rurik Valdren as a possible counterweight because Rurik wants dwarven leads and wealth but may be more controllable than the military or churches. At this point, contacting Rurik is only a plan; the transcript says temple events must happen before Jefferson can act.

## Session 8: Ssar'Velyn Temple And Bartholomew Hildebrant

Session 8 begins with Bartholomew Hildebrant waking from a void-state in the middle of a Fourth-Age temple assault. He is badly wounded, poisoned, and concussed. Katrina, a wood elf cleric, heals him while also badly hurt. The room contains shattered giant spider eggs, dead huge spiders, a dead orc wizard named Oscar, and dead drow attackers. Katrina is killed by an invisible assassin. Bartholomew fights through the remaining defenders, has his knees ruined, kills the assassin, and crawls into a stasis pod with his axe and crossbow rather than surrender.

In the present, the party leaves Hollowmere with the Sunhollow escort: Havlin, Penelope, Voss, and Reggie. Bevar is increasingly strange: short with the party, focused on Gwen, listening to her, and hiding more than he says. The party again recognizes that the approaching church/Inquisition/mercenary force plus valley ether wealth is a dangerous combination.

At the edge of the Dead Lands, the ground becomes ash-like. The blight appears as oily black strands like greasy webbing. The smell is concentrated poison, old ash, and burned flesh. An ancient elven ghost druid appears near a well and guides the party. She was once tied to the forest and became corrupted when it did.

The ghost druid gives old history. The valley was once a meeting place for all elven peoples under one god, not originally a Lolth-only site. In the Second Age, fey losing against giants cursed them into dwarves; the dwarves retaliated by breaking the world and bringing in the first dragon. That dragon remained on a mountain for two ages. Lolth later allied with that dragon and the Weaver, killed a god, stole divine power, betrayed the elven treaty halls, and corrupted the holy ground. A later crusade drove her out. The ghost recognizes Jefferson's amulet as power tied to one of the killed divinities, Hestia.

The temple approach contains mosaics and old elven/drow-spider language that Alistair recognizes from Bevar's book. The mosaics tell Lolth's fall narrative: beauty and peace rejected, pain embraced, spiders studied and bred, transformation pursued, and the Weaver met as the fey betrayer who helped bring the dragon into the world.

At the temple, Bevar insists the job is to fix the corruption, not just identify it. Gwen appears to guide him. Jose and Jefferson challenge him, but he moves forward. A ghost priestess resembling Selyra Vex'ryn appears and demands to know why males tread holy ground. Wisdom saves fail and the party freezes. Bevar kneels, says they are humble servants of a mistress, and Gwen is recognized as emissary. The priestess says the emissary will follow her while the others must complete trials to leave alive.

Jefferson tries to intervene and falls through the floor. This separates him from the visible trial sequence.

Jose and Alistair avoid the worst of the trap and enter side rooms. They recover books, porcelain, potions, brewing supplies, poisoner's tools, artificer/enchanting tools, and magically sealed jewelry boxes. They descend into a stasis chamber like the Kex medbay but older and different. Most pods contain dead drow or older assault-team corpses. One pod contains Bartholomew alive but frozen in his broken state.

Jose opens the pod and the party stabilizes Bartholomew with a greater healing potion. His knees remain ruined. He joins by riding on Jose's shoulders, creating an accidental mounted-combat setup.

The temple trials require three living participants and count Jose, Alistair, and Bartholomew. The first round pits them against drow guardians including an assassin, monk, summoner, and warlock/sorcerer. Bartholomew, mounted on Jose, becomes brutally lethal, beheading the monk and helping clear the field. Jose uses mobility, grappling, shotgun pressure, and Hornet. Alistair reads roles and uses ranged attacks.

The temple says they have earned the right to leave alive, but they continue. The second round features casters: lightning sorcerer, summoner, necromancer, and another caster. Alistair shoots a crystal and causes an explosion/backlash. Bartholomew kills the lightning sorcerer. Jose uses the shotgun at close range to knock a caster off platform and break concentration. Jose later throws Bartholomew so the dwarf can kill the summoner mid-flight.

Loot includes the summoner staff, purple potion, necromancer robes, spider bracelet, twin bloodsteel daggers, shroud of invisibility, widow's bracelet, health potions, belt of elven quickness, bracers of defense, arcane focus, spider ammo, and later the Dark Drow Archer Set. Some item cards remain unfinished.

The final optional trial brings a massive drider or spider-bodied temple guardian. She fights from the ceiling, uses webbing, Shield, a large mace, and shifting initiative. Alistair cuts free with an ether conductor. Bartholomew lands major critical damage. The guardian stops the fight and declares them passed.

As reward, the guardian returns Jefferson in a cocoon. She heals the party to full through blood magic, sacrificing her own blood into a symbol. Alistair recognizes this from Bevar's book.

The true moral choice follows. The emissary has told the guardian about outsiders following the party. The valley inhabitants cannot leave because of the blood oath to her ancestors and may be bound to defend the temple. The following church/Inquisition/mercenary force will likely kill or exploit the valley for what it contains. Bartholomew's old assault nearly destroyed the temple, leaving few guardians. Destroying the temple may kill thousands of bound innocents; letting outsiders come may also doom them.

The guardian notes rabbits are not all bound the same way. She notices Bartholomew's medallion and Jefferson's matching Hestia amulet, finds it interesting, and teleports the party outside. Bevar and Gwen remain with or inside the temple for unresolved emissary work.

Key exports from Session 8:

- Bartholomew Hildebrant joins from the Fourth Age.
- Ssar'Velyn is a temple/forest/oath system, not just a dungeon.
- Lolth, Hestia, the first dragon, and the Weaver are tied to the old history.
- Bevar and Gwen are separated from the party.
- The valley's oath-bound inhabitants may be forced to defend the temple.
- The party now has a political and moral crisis, not just a combat problem.

## Session 9: Vex'ryn's Warning And The Inquisition Countdown

Session 9 resolves Jefferson's separated route. Instead of falling through the intended path, he activates the plasma greatblade, stabs it into the shaft wall, melts/slows his descent, and cuts into hidden temple spaces.

Jefferson explores an abandoned route containing a broken spider plinth, shattered egg or crypt chamber, a mechanism with a missing lever/key/handle, a vast vat chamber full of thick purple demonic ichor, connected pipes, unreadable archives, and an office that Selyra Vex'ryn calls her mother's office. The ichor burns through leather but holds in treated glass, so Jefferson collects a demonic ichor vial.

Inside the office, Jefferson meets Mistress Selyra Vex'ryn, or a manifestation presenting as her. She says the temple was dedicated to Lolth's path to ascension. She claims the drow faction once won the valley, the temple has been lost to most of her people for more than two ages, Bartholomew's old assault damaged the vats and defenses, and Lolth is not truly gone while a remnant remains.

Selyra also claims that Hestia is not gone because hearth, home, welcome, motherhood, and hospitality still act as unconscious worship. She frames Hestia as part of Lolth's ascension system rather than merely as a defeated rival.

Most importantly, Selyra claims the immediate corruption source is not the temple itself, but something imprisoned in a cave south along the wall. She says she needs one to two weeks without the temple being disturbed and warns that if the Church or Inquisition threatens the temple before her work is done, old oaths and old magics may kill many people.

Jefferson's insight reads her as layered and dangerous. Some of what she says is likely true, some omitted, and some possibly lies. The cave-source claim should therefore remain a claim or partial truth, not settled canon.

Selyra webs or transports Jefferson back to the others outside the temple. Bevar is not with them. The party recognizes that Bevar's behavior had become strange and that Gwen, the temple, the spider presence, or Bevar's own choices may all be involved.

The party rests, compares accounts, and examines loot. The potion cache includes blue mana/spell restoration potions with exhaustion cost, a stronger blue potion, a purple spider mutation/stat boost potion with later permanent stat cost, a deadly green ingested poison, and a larger drow/spider/sacrifice transformation potion. Counts and final item names remain unsettled.

Jefferson contacts the Inquisition through a communication stone or amulet. Communication had failed deeper in the valley, but now connects. The contact is [Lord Inquisitor Amir Voss](../people/amir-voss.md), captioned as "Air Voss." The Inquisition says they are about two days out from the party's last marker. Jefferson gives careful partial truths: the locals are not immediately hostile, the situation is complicated, the far side may be questionable, and communications failed after entering the valley. He does not fully reveal the temple.

Jefferson then contacts Rurik Valdren, activating the Session 7 idea of using Rurik as a shield. He pitches the valley as an enormous opportunity, asking what it would be worth to secure something on the scale of a world. Rurik becomes intrigued by the ether wealth, bound inhabitants, drow/divine/demonic stakes, the approaching Inquisition, and Bartholomew as an ancient dwarf who can speak Dwarven.

Rurik speaks with Bartholomew and presses old identity clues: Hildebrant, banners, halls, tattoos, possible banishment, military history. He also warns Jefferson that Selyra's word "release" is dangerous in blood magic. Release could mean freedom, death, dismemberment, fulfillment, or another oath-defined end.

Bartholomew's Hestia connection deepens. When Hestia's name is spoken, the soul-pain inside him fades for the first time he can remember. Near Jefferson's pendant and his own medallion, the presence feels like a campfire in the void. Later he prays and receives warmth, comfort, and bodily strengthening.

The party debates whether to go straight to the cave Selyra named. Jefferson argues against rushing. The corruption has persisted for thousands of years, while the Inquisition is approaching within days. The party chooses to return to the council, create a political shield, and use Rurik if possible before dealing with the cave.

They reconnect with the Sunhollow escort and move quickly back toward the council. Along the way, Bartholomew receives a crash course in the post-Concurrence world: Unity, the remade worlds, the Astral Sea, ley lines, the Citadel, the hollow world, the Underdark/Upside Down, dwarves, drow, and broader history.

The party reaches the council area at Sunhollow, the old elven conclave site. Jefferson recognizes that the valley was an elven stronghold whose defenses may still be meant to admit elves, not outsiders. They see the scale of the population more clearly: tens of thousands of animalfolk and many children. This reinforces the danger if old defenses or oath consequences are triggered.

The session ends before the council meeting. At first contact, the Inquisition was about two days out. After the party's return travel, the effective countdown is closer to about one day before the Inquisition reaches the gate.

Key exports from Session 9:

- Jefferson collects a demonic ichor vial.
- Selyra Vex'ryn is directly involved in the temple crisis or can manifest there.
- Selyra's cave-source and one-to-two-week claims are unconfirmed.
- Bevar remains missing after following Gwen.
- Rurik is now actively involved, not just a possible option.
- Bartholomew's Hestia link becomes active and restorative.
- The campaign becomes a political countdown.
