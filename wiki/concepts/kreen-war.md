# The Kreen War

The Kreen War is a **Seventh-Age** conflict, not a Sixth-Age or pre-Concurrence event. It begins after [the Concurrence](the-concurrence.md), when the linked worlds are already learning to live with changed magic, new travel, and weakened divine intervention.

## Opening of the War

The invasion enters through a gate-region on [Driftvale](../places/driftvale.md), later associated with the [Gorbin Ruins](../places/gorbin-ruins.md). Driftvale is the first gateway-frontier: the Kreen establish themselves in the cleared territory while the deep fog remains dangerous to them.

Within weeks, Driftvale is nearly overrun. Within months, Kreen attacks spread across multiple worlds. By the opening of [Session 1](../sessions/session-1.md), the war has lasted roughly a year and the dead are estimated in the tens of millions. The gods do not intervene.

## The Bloody Fifth and the Gate Battle

The campaign opens during the war's last desperate phase. The [Bloody Fifth](../factions/bloody-fifth.md) is sent to hold and pressure the Kreen line on Driftvale while allied heroes strike the reinforcement portal.

The Fifth's assault creates the opening the heroes need to close the portal. A psychic or magical shockwave follows. Kreen on the battlefield collapse, flee, fight one another, or become disoriented; the table treats this as evidence that closing the portal severed their hive connection for the first time.

The battle breaks the invasion's main reinforcement line and marks the war's effective end. It does not make every Kreen problem disappear: the [Unified Armed Forces](../factions/unified-armed-forces.md) form [Ash's Hounds](../factions/ashs-hounds.md) for the postwar period, to locate rogue groups, prevent isolated queens or equivalent leaders from rebuilding control, and capture Kreen when possible.

## Surrender, Captivity, and the War's Moral Cost

The post-gate campaign reveals that the Kreen cannot be understood only as a single enemy mass. Some disconnected Kreen surrender, communicate, or protect civilians. The events at [Norinar](../places/norinar.md) force the party to confront that fact directly.

Military capture programs later enable the [Kreen Control Collars](../items/kreen-control-collars.md) and a wider surrender policy. They also rely on coercion and experimentation, leaving the war's political end morally compromised rather than cleanly heroic.
