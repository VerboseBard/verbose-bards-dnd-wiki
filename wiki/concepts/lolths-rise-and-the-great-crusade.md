# Lolth's Rise and the Great Crusade

Lolth's rise and the Great Crusade form one of the central conflicts of the [Fourth Age](fourth-age.md). The surviving account is assembled from the [Dead Lands Ghost Druid](../people/dead-lands-ghost-druid.md), [Ssar'Velyn Temple](../places/ssar-velyn-temple.md), [Rurik Valdren](../people/rurik-valdren.md), [Bartholomew Hildebrant](../people/bartholomew-hildebrant.md), and later Church research. None of those witnesses provides a complete omniscient history, so disputed motives and incomplete counts remain labeled.

## Lolth's Ascent

The oldest account describes Lolth as a luxury-born drow who rejected the elven conclave's ideal of beauty as a lie. She adopted pain as the truest form of existence, took the spider as her model, and pursued power through spider breeding, transformation, and forbidden study.

Lolth allied with [the First Dragon](../people/first-dragon.md). Temple mosaics and the ghost druid also place [the Weaver](the-weaver.md), a betrayer associated with foresight, behind the arrangement. With that aid, Lolth killed a god and stole its power, becoming the first known mortal to commit deicide and take divinity for herself.

She then entered the shared elven holy valley, where violence had been impossible, betrayed the conclave, drove rival elves out, and corrupted its sacred sites into the system now centered on [Ssar'Velyn Temple](../places/ssar-velyn-temple.md).

## Drow Resistance and the Great Crusade

Lolth did not command all drow. Surviving testimony says many drow fought against her alongside other elven peoples and the wider crusading coalition. The current record does not preserve a single formal name, leader, or exact chronology for a unified "drow rebellion," so the wiki treats this as confirmed anti-Lolth drow resistance within the larger Great Crusade rather than inventing a separate organization.

The Great Crusade answered Lolth's deicide and seizure of the valley. It drove her from the shared holy ground, but her cult survived and later turned to summoning demons and devils. The gods and mortal powers then organized simultaneous strikes against her remaining places of power.

## The Temple Network

Bartholomew's recovered [Session 11](../sessions/session-11.md) memory shows thirteen planning tables, each assigned to a temple. A Justicar's report in that memory listed nine sites destroyed and two more never accounted for. When the surviving Sunhollow site is included, the spoken figures account for only twelve locations, leaving one thirteenth site unexplained.

The [Ssar'Velyn Temple](../places/ssar-velyn-temple.md) assault used roughly ten mixed teams and about forty soldiers. Bartholomew's Slayer Team was one part of that wider operation. The cult was mostly destroyed, but Lolth vanished and was believed dead.

## Hestia and the Conversion System

[Hestia](../people/hestia.md), goddess of hearth and refuge, was among the divinities believed killed during Lolth's rise. Later Church doctrine says Lolth enslaved Hestia fragments recovered from eight destroyed temples. Demons absorbed divine power, and installations such as the [Demon-Draining Engine](demon-draining-engine.md) converted that power into a form mortals could use.

The Fourth-Age raids starved Lolth's system for two ages but did not fully destroy it. Hestia endured because acts of hearth, home, welcome, and care continued to sustain her even when worshippers no longer knew her name.

## Aftermath

On the same day as the coordinated temple strikes, the First Dragon rampaged and was killed by the surviving allied forces. The shamed drow later spread toward [Saharun](../places/saharun.md) and the great woods, while surviving Lolth cults periodically resurfaced.

By the end of the Fifth Age, a drow purchaser acquired survivors of the failed leonin slave uprising and brought them into the valley. Their descendants became part of Sunhollow's inherited blood curse, linking Fourth-Age divine history to the present [Leonin Curse-Breaking Ritual](leonin-curse-breaking-ritual.md).
