# Third Age

The Third Age begins after the [Fey](fey.md) withdraw from the material world. It is defined by the failed dwarven attempt to destroy [the First Dragon](../people/first-dragon.md), the creation of the [Dead Lands](../places/dead-lands.md), the first great defensive lines against the undead, and a direct rewriting of reality that binds dragonkind to the world's survival.

## The Failed Dragon War

The dwarves accepted responsibility for the calamity their ancestors had unleashed and attempted to kill the First Dragon. They failed. The attack provoked a rampage lasting nearly fifty years, killing millions and turning a vast section of the continent into the Dead Lands.

The devastation was worsened by uncontrolled eleventh- and twelfth-stage magic. When the dragon finally returned to its mountain, the dead did not remain still. Undead armies rose from the ruined lands and pressed outward.

## The Lines of Survival

The dwarves treated containment as penance. They built walls, fortified the mountain passes, and fought continuously against the undead. It took roughly 180 years to stabilize a connected series of defensive positions.

The surviving line was not dwarven alone:

- The elven lands held behind divine and magical defenses woven through their forests.
- The sea contained much of the undead advance along the coast.
- Humans and other peoples held another forest line with divine aid.
- A shifting corridor of roughly 200 to 250 miles of unpolluted coast was preserved until it linked with the elven lands.
- Dwarves held the mountain passes and the great remaining stretch around the Vale, including the defenses that became [the Dragon Watch](../places/dragon-watch.md).

These defenses became the foundation of the walls, orders, and military culture that endured into the [Fourth Age](fourth-age.md).

## Big Shad's Sacrifice

The wider world also suffered famine and social collapse. When starvation among the elves became extreme, [Big Shad](big-shad.md), remembered in this tradition as the Great Shad, willingly sacrificed himself to feed and preserve his people.

This is remembered as the first time a divinity, even a lesser divinity, willingly died to rescue the people who worshiped him.

## The Rewriting of Reality

No reliable record identifies the person who performed the Third Age's great rewriting. Somehow, an unknown power harnessed the accumulated death energy and changed several laws of reality. It was one of the last direct rewritings of the world's governing rules before [the Breaking](the-breaking.md) at the end of the Fifth Age.

The rewrite:

- bound the lives of the dragon guardians to the stability of reality;
- reinforced an early divine barrier that prevented gods from intervening freely in the material world;
- restricted ascension so that only existing gods, their children, or beings already possessing equivalent power could reach true godhood;
- established one active guardian place for each draconic race or color; and
- placed unhatched replacements into stasis until a corresponding dragon died and suitable hatching conditions existed.

[Tiamat](../people/tiamat.md) became the Mother of Dragons, while her brother became their protector. If a required egg could not hatch, the missing guardian left reality's defenses weakened until conditions became suitable.

The full identity and motive of the rewriter remain unknown. Later evidence raises the possibility that [the Weaver](the-weaver.md) manipulated the rewriter or revealed the method, but this remains suspicion rather than settled fact.

## Long Consequences

The dragon-bound defense eventually became the weakness [Lucius](../people/lucius.md) exploited at the end of the [Fifth Age](fifth-age.md). By arranging the deaths of the dragon guardians, he could weaken the barrier, break the old ascension restrictions, shatter the world, and destroy the older Divine Gate structure.
