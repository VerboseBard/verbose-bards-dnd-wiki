# Lord Inquisitor Amir Voss

Lord Inquisitor Amir Voss is the tall aasimar [Inquisition](../factions/inquisition.md) authority in white robes of office who leads the Inquisition force operating on [Driftvale](../places/driftvale.md), and later the Inquisition element approaching [Sunhollow Valley](../places/sunhollow-valley.md).

## Name Note

Earlier records rendered his name from caption drift as `Boss`, `Bosh`, or `Bos`, and the Session 9 sending-stone contact as `Air Voss`. The independent Whisper transcript hears `Lord Voss` (Session 4) and `Amir Voss` (Session 9), matching the planning notes' `Aamir Voss`. Per GM ruling, the Session 4 Lord Inquisitor is the head of the Inquisition force the party is dealing with, and his name is **Amir Voss**. The legacy page [Lord Inquisitor Boss](lord-inquisitor-boss.md) now points here.

Whether he is related to the other Voss figures in the record ([Sabine Voss](sabine-voss.md), [Captain Darel Voss](captain-darel-voss.md), [Sergeant Halric Voss](sergeant-halric-voss.md)) is unconfirmed.

## Role

He represents the terrifying side of the [Inquisition](../factions/inquisition.md): truth magic, public punishment, and uncompromising suspicion.

## Key Actions

- Uses the [Truth Lantern](../items/truth-lantern.md) to test whether the party was truthful in their report to [Captain Hail](captain-hail.md) in [Session 4](../sessions/session-4.md).
- Publicly executes [Colonel Marrow Vance](colonel-marrow-vance.md) and other corrupt officers/non-coms with light/fire bindings.
- Remains suspicious of the party even after [Colonel Core](colonel-core.md) closes the inquiry under the code of conduct.
- In [Session 9](../sessions/session-9.md), answers Jefferson's sending stone as the Inquisition contact: the force is about two days out from the party's last marker, and he asks pointed questions about corruption, heresy, and the fate of the church's scout.

## Rurik's Assessment

In [Session 9](../sessions/session-9.md), [Rurik Valdren](rurik-valdren.md) describes the man running the Inquisition as a zealot with a very simple outlook: if it benefits the church, it is good, and whoever stands in the way must therefore be evil. Rurik says he "should have been one of the penitent, not an Inquisitor," and considers the expedition's Justicar the more decent authority.

## Sunhollow Expedition

In [Session 10](../sessions/session-10.md), Amir arrives in person and nearly turns the valley gate into another confrontation with Rurik. [The Justicar](the-justicar.md) accepts Owlin guest-right, recognizes Rurik's current legal protection, and orders Amir to respect it. The Justicar later describes Amir as useful and sincere but increasingly clouded by greed; that is the Justicar's assessment, not an objective verdict.

In [Session 11](../sessions/session-11.md), Amir uses the [Truth Lantern](../items/truth-lantern.md) to obtain Bartholomew's complete signed testimony. He confirms that the signature on [his conditional execution writ](../items/amir-voss-execution-writ.md) is genuine and explains that Inquisitors accept such warrants as part of the Church's reciprocal watcher system.

Amir prepares legal action against Rurik and regards Sunhollow's ether as leverage needed by a Church weakened after the War of the Gods. On the march, he releases the Dead Lands ghost druid, manifests wings at the demon complex, and draws power through his bodyguard. He also proposes killing the Hadozee contingent before its expected compelled attack. Jefferson agrees, and Church forces and mercenaries carry out the strike before the Hadozee act.
