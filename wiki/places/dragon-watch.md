# The Dragon Watch

The Dragon Watch was the named military station and command on the enormous dwarven wall that sealed off the undead wastelands. The defensive system began in the [Third Age](../concepts/third-age.md) and remained central through the Fourth and Fifth Ages. The physical fortification can also be called the Great Wall; Dragon Watch was the posting where [Bartholomew Hildebrant](../people/bartholomew-hildebrant.md) served under Colonel [Rurik Valdren](../people/rurik-valdren.md).

## Description

A wall on the scale of legend, garrisoned and fighting twenty-four hours a day: undead hordes out of the wastes, demons that manifested along the line, wasteland monstrosities, and at the heights the ancient dragon threat itself. Dwarven military culture ran through it: every dwarf out of training served a six-month rotation on the wall before anything else.

## Third-Age Founding

The dwarves created the First Dragon catastrophe in the Second Age. When they later attempted to kill the dragon, they failed and provoked a nearly fifty-year rampage. Millions died, uncontrolled eleventh- and twelfth-stage magic scarred the continent, and the vast [Dead Lands](dead-lands.md) were created.

After the dragon returned to its mountain, undead armies began emerging from the devastated region. It took roughly 180 years for the dwarves and their allies to stabilize a connected defense.

The line was shared among the surviving peoples:

- Dwarves held the mountain passes and the great remaining stretch around the Vale.
- Elven lands held behind magical and divine forest defenses.
- The sea contained much of the undead movement along the coast.
- Humans and other peoples held another forest line through divine protection.
- A shifting corridor of roughly 200 to 250 miles of unpolluted coast survived until it connected with the elven lands.

The Dragon Watch grew from the dwarven portion of this system.

## Penance

The dwarves held the line for roughly 4,500 years as penance. Their permanent war against the wastes forged the discipline that later supported the dwarven hegemony. Per Rurik, this is also why the dwarves conquered the world in the Fourth and Fifth Ages: an entire civilization shaped by the wall.

The order that manned it was [the Sentinels of the Veil](../factions/sentinels-of-the-veil.md).

## Fall

Rurik commanded a section of the wall for two hundred years and was standing on it when the Breaking came. With the world shattered and the defenders scattered, most believed the old Vale or Veil regions fell to the undead. What remains of the wall in the Seventh Age, and what still stalks the far side of it, is unknown.
